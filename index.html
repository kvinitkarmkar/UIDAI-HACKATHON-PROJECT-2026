02

The "State" dimension flaw

India consists of 28 states and 8 union territories. The raw dataset arrived with 65 unique state-level entries — a high-cardinality mess of case errors, spelling drift, renamed states, and outright noise that had to be reconciled before any statistic could be trusted.

A · Case & phonetic variation

West Bengal · WEST BENGAL · west Bengal · Westbengal · West Bangal — and Odisha vs. ODISHA vs. the archaic Orissa.

B · Syntactic & whitespace noise

Double-spaced entries like West Bengal, and delimiter drift between Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Dadra and Nagar Haveli.

C · Temporal & administrative outliers

Legacy names Uttaranchal and Pondicherry persist alongside their current forms; Jammu & Kashmir still appears as a state despite its UT reclassification.

D · Extraneous, non-categorical noise

City-level leakage — Nagpur, Jaipur, Darbhanga, Balanagar — plus stray numeric strings like 100000 sitting inside the state field.

03

Cleaning impact

Exact row-level matching across all six demographic attributes — date, state, district, pincode, and both age fields — surfaced 474,307 genuine duplicate rows, 22.89% of the raw dataset. The deduplication pipeline preserved first occurrence and produced 1,597,393 trustworthy records.

Why large demographic databases drift

Multi-source collection

Records ingested from parallel enrolment channels without a shared identity key.

Regional partitioning overlaps

State-wise data slices occasionally re-capture the same citizen record.

System replication errors

Sync retries across centres write the same update more than once.

1 · Multi-state aggregation 2 · Exact row matching 3 · Pattern frequency 4 · Geo validation 5 · Statistical verification 6 · First-occurrence retention 7 · Audit trail 8 · QA checks

Strategy A

Infrastructure & load management

Target: Central (9.9M) · East (8.4M) · South (6.4M)

Invest in infrastructure expansion and processing capacity to manage high organic demand in developed, densely-populated regions.

  • Permanent centres & mini-hubs in high-density districts
  • Backend system upgrades for faster processing
  • Faster DBT delivery, lower per-update cost
Strategy B

Awareness & inclusion expansion

Target: North · West · Northeast

Increase awareness, accessibility, and trust in regions with lower update penetration.

  • Mobile enrolment units & temporary camps
  • Localised campaigns through schools & local offices
  • Cleaner databases, fewer exclusion errors
